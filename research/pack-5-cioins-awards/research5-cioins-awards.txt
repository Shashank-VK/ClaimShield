CASE 1 — Bharat Hirji Dedhia vs Union Of India And Anr
  Forum: Bombay High Court (Ordinary Original Civil Jurisdiction)
  Case Number: WP No. 2903 of 2021 & WP No. 706 of 2024; IA No. 424 of 2022
  Date: 9 December 2024
  Judge: M.S. Sonak, J. & Jitendra Jain, J.
  Indian Kanoon Doc ID: doc/180108982
  URL: https://indiankanoon.org/doc/180108982/
  Insurer: The Oriental Insurance Company Ltd.
  Diagnosis Group: Coronary artery disease (CAD); ischemic heart disease; diabetes mellitus
  Rejection Ground & Clause Cited: (1) Non-disclosure of pre-existing disease (ischemic heart disease from 14.5.2016; diabetes mellitus) in proposal/self-declaration form — alleged policy void; (2) Reasonable and Customary (R&C) charges — Clause 3.41 of Oriental Insurance Happy Family Floater Policy 2015 ("reasonable and customary charges" = charges standard and consistent with prevailing charges in geographical area for identical/similar services, taking into account nature of illness/injury). Insurer claimed surgeon's charges (Rs.21,87,500/-) were excessive vs 3 other CAD patients at Breach Candy Hospital.
  Documents Claimant Produced: Hospital/surgeon payment evidence (unimpeachable per claimant's counsel); medical records; insurance policies; award document dated 03 May 2021.
  Ombudsman Award: Ombudsman award dated 03 May 2021 directed Oriental Insurance to pay Rs.27,13,582/- against health insurance policy. Claim was Rs.37,02,293/-; insurer had paid only Rs.5,81,466/-. Partial repudiation existed → Ombudsman had jurisdiction under Clause 13(1)(b) of IRDA Notification 25.4.2017.
  Reasoning: (1) Proposal form unsigned, never raised before Ombudsman (afterthought); after accepting premium and issuing policy, insurer cannot deny validity. (2) R&C challenge failed — insurer did not dispute that Rs.21,87,500/- was paid; comparative chart insufficient without evidence of other patients' health conditions; Clause 3.41 requires considering nature of illness/injury. (3) Award of Rs.27,13,582/- was within policy terms after accounting for multiple policies. (4) Insurer suppressed email dated 17.6.2021 acknowledging payment processing; failed to comply with award within 30 days (Clause 17(6) IRDA Notification); delayed clearing office objections for 3+ years.
  Outcome: Writ Petition No. 706 of 2024 DISMISSED with exemplary costs of Rs.1,00,000/-; interest at 7% p.a. on awarded amount from 01 July 2021; costs and interest recoverable from responsible officials; compliance report due by 16 June 2025.
  Rupee Amounts: Claim Rs.37,02,293/-; awarded Rs.27,13,582/-; insurer paid Rs.5,81,466/-; surgeon charges Rs.21,87,500/-; exemplary costs Rs.1,00,000/-; interest 7% p.a.
  Pattern: Non-payable items / R&C cuts — REASONABLE AND CUSTOMARY CHARGES (Clause 3.41)

CASE 2 — HDFC ERGO General Insurance Company Limited vs The Insurance Ombudsman
  Forum: Calcutta High Court (Appellate Side)
  Case Number: WPA 13482 of 2021
  Date: 12 April 2022
  Judge: Rajasekhar Mantha, J.
  Indian Kanoon Doc ID: doc/194387847
  URL: https://indiankanoon.org/doc/194387847/
  Insurer: HDFC ERGO General Insurance Company Ltd.
  Diagnosis Group: Tumor (possibly cancerous) — health insurance claim
  Rejection Ground & Clause Cited: Repudiation of health insurance claim; Ombudsman found repudiation prima facie unjustified. Insurer challenged on: (1) violation of natural justice (independent medical opinion not furnished to insurer); (2) complaint bad in law — Rule 14(2) Insurance Ombudsman Rules 2017 (nature and extent of loss not specified); (3) award vague — Rule 17(3) (no quantified compensation); (4) award beyond 3-month period — Rule 17(4).
  Documents Claimant Produced: Claim documents lodged with insurance company containing entire hospital expenditure.
  Ombudsman Award: Order dated 17 June 2021 by Insurance Ombudsman, Kolkata — directed HDFC ERGO to honour the claim; complaint disposed in favour of complainant (Pabitra Kumar Gayen).
  Reasoning: (1) Ombudsman proceedings are summary in nature; strict CPC/evidence rules don't apply, but natural justice principles are inherent (Rule 15). (2) Rule 15(3) allows Ombudsman to obtain expert opinion; no obligation to circulate it. (3) Insurer waived entitlement by not producing counter-opinion. (4) Absence of quantified claim amount not a serious infraction. (5) 3-month rule under Rule 17(4) to be interpreted liberally/purposively (beneficial legislation); delay not fatal. (6) Judicial review scope under Article 226 is very limited — awards not amenable unless patently unfair, perverse, jurisdictional infirmity, or natural justice violation.
  Outcome: Writ petition DISMISSED; no order as to costs. Ombudsman's award upheld.
  Rupee Amounts: Not quantified in award (vague per insurer's challenge).
  Pattern: General health insurance claim repudiation; procedural fairness at Ombudsman level

CASE 3 — Care Health Insurance Ltd vs Manjula Haresh Joisar And Anr
  Forum: Bombay High Court (Civil Appellate Jurisdiction)
  Case Number: WP No. 9028 of 2024; 2026:BHC-AS:5149
  Date: 23 January 2026
  Judge: Somasekhar Sundaresan, J.
  Indian Kanoon Doc ID: doc/198677256
  URL: https://indiankanoon.org/doc/198677256/
  Insurer: Care Health Insurance Ltd. (formerly Star Health and Allied Insurance Co. Ltd.)
  Diagnosis Group: Carcinoma of tonsils (non-healing patches diagnosed as carcinoma; chemotherapy in 2021) — health insurance claim after policy porting/migration
  Rejection Ground & Clause Cited: Non-disclosure of carcinoma (pre-existing disease) at time of policy porting. Care Health repudiated claim dated 25 April 2022 citing non-disclosure. Issue: whether new insurer (Care Health), having accepted porting and premium, is deemed to have full notice of entire medical/claim history of insured before porting decision.
  Documents Claimant Produced: Policy porting documents; claim history from Star Health; medical records of carcinoma diagnosis and chemotherapy.
  Ombudsman Award: Award dated 7 May 2024 — Ombudsman held onus of ascertaining entire claim history when porting a policy lies on the insurance company.
  Reasoning: (1) IRDA (Health Insurance) Regulations 2016, Regulation 17 + Schedule-I govern portability. (2) New insurer has reasonable advance period (60 days prior to 45 days prior to expiry) to assess claim history. (3) Existing insurer must furnish data on IRDAI web portal within 7 working days. (4) If new insurer doesn't communicate decision within 15 days of receiving data, it loses right to reject. (5) Care Health accepted porting and premium without receiving claim data — cannot blame dysfunctional IIB portal; could have rejected porting request. (6) Having accepted porting and premium, insurer is presumed to have taken informed decision.
  Outcome: Petition DISMISSED; Ombudsman's award upheld (no interference).
  Rupee Amounts: Not specified in judgment text.
  Pattern: Pre-existing disease / non-disclosure; policy porting duty of new insurer

CASE 4 — Mrs Mamata Satpathy vs The Zonal Manager LIC Of India And Others
  Forum: Orissa High Court, Cuttack
  Case Number: W.P. (C) No. 5265 of 2009; AIR 2017 ORISSA 54
  Date: 16 January 2017
  Judge: Dr. Justice D.P. Choudhury
  Indian Kanoon Doc ID: doc/120168296
  URL: https://indiankanoon.org/doc/120168296/
  Insurer: Life Insurance Corporation of India (LIC)
  Diagnosis Group: Life insurance — death due to septicaemia (primary) and drug rash (secondary); Ombudsman relied on bed-head ticket noting "Seizure Disorder" since 1995
  Rejection Ground & Clause Cited: Suppression of material facts — deceased policy-holder allegedly suppressed illness (seizure disorder) at time of proposal/revival (2002, 2004). Section 45 of Insurance Act, 1938 invoked. Ombudsman Complaint No. 21-001-0235; Ombudsman order dated 27.8.2008 accepted insurer's submissions and rejected claim.
  Documents Claimant Produced: Legal heir certificate; discharge summary; Annexure-3 (hospital death form showing septicaemia as provisional and final diagnosis); Annexure-7 (medical attendant's reply negative about other diseases); medical examiner's confidential reports (policies 584766253 and 585424076 — all answers "No" to hospitalization, surgery, tests, treatment).
  Ombudsman Award: Insurance Ombudsman rejected complaint (order dated 27.8.2008) — held claim repudiated due to suppression of material facts based on bed-head ticket endorsement.
  Reasoning: (1) Section 45(2) — after 2 years, policy can only be called in question if statement was on material matter, fraudulently made, and policy-holder knew it was false. (2) Onus on insurer to prove all three conditions. (3) Medical examiner's reports (dated 31.8.2002 and 31.3.2004) showed doctor found no infirmity; all answers "No". (4) Bed-head ticket endorsement about "Seizure Disorder" not proven — no doctor examined to prove it; no hospitalization for it. (5) Cause of death was septicaemia, not seizure disorder. (6) Ombudsman's finding based on no evidence → quashed.
  Outcome: Writ petition allowed; Ombudsman's order quashed; LIC directed to release claim amount with interest @ 9% p.a. from 3.11.2005 (date of repudiation) till actual payment within 3 months; failing which 12% p.a.
  Rupee Amounts: Claim amount under Policies Nos. 584766253 and 585424076 (not specified in text); interest @ 9% p.a. from 3.11.2005.
  Pattern: Suppression of material facts / pre-existing disease — life insurance; Section 45 Insurance Act
